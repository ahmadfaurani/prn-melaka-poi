## PRN Negeri Sembilan ke-16: Results Overview

| Coalition | Seats Won |
|-----------|-----------|
| Barisan Nasional (BN) | 18 (highest) |
| Pakatan Harapan (PH) | 11 |
| Perikatan Nasional (PN) | 7 |

- No single party/coalition achieved simple majority of 19 seats
- New Menteri Besar: Datuk Ismail Lasim (ADUN Juasseh, BN) — selected through BN-PN cooperation with two-thirds mandate in Dewan Undangan Negeri

## Election Surprises

> "Loke tewas di Chennah selepas 13 tahun menjadi ADUN di kawasan itu, manakala Aminuddin kalah di kubu kuat BN di Linggi."

- Anthony Loke (DAP Secretary General) — lost in Chennah after 13 years as ADUN
- Datuk Seri Aminuddin Harun (PH Negeri Sembilan Chairman) — lost in Linggi

## Key Factors & Analysis

### BN-PN Cooperation
- Avoiding multi-cornered fights between BN and PN was a major winning factor
- Even first-time candidates from Parti Wawasan Negara (on PN tickets) won in 3 constituencies
- However, cooperation still too early to be declared "accepted by the people"
- This is BN's second PRN victory after Johor; PRN Melaka is next

### PH's Position
- PH's total votes did not significantly decline
- PH obtained the highest overall vote share — approximately 40%
- Voter loyalty remained, but insufficient to win under the current electoral system
- PH needs to review and redesign its election strategy

### Warning to BN

> "Tidak boleh leka dan selesa. Apatah lagi BN masih belum mampu menawan hati sebahagian pengundi kaum Cina."

- BN must not be complacent
- BN still unable to win over a segment of Chinese voters

## Changing Voter Landscape

> "Pengundi hari ini bukan seperti 20 tahun lalu. Mereka bukan menerima maklumat dari satu sumber sahaja."

- No safe seats exist anymore for any party
- Even prominent political figures can lose if they: Take local issues lightly, Are insensitive to current sentiments
- Young voters can shift allegiance at any time

## Core Thesis: Governing vs. Opposition

> "Nampak macam, lebih mudah menjadi pembangkang daripada jadi kerajaan."

Challenges of being in government today:
- Managing and developing the state/country
- Managing perception and sentiment
- Managing both friends and foes
- The public is always watching

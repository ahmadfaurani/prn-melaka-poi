## Key Developments

- **Amanah will focus on strengthening political cooperation within Pakatan Harapan (PH)** while exploring broader collaboration with parties sharing its commitment to values-based politics
- Strategic shift follows the party's poor performance in the **Johor and Negri Sembilan state elections**
- **Amanah failed to win any of the 9 seats it contested** in the Negri Sembilan election, including the **Klawang state seat**, which it had previously held (now WAWASAN per separate file)

## Key Quotes from Vice-President Adly Zahari

> "PH will remain the cornerstone of Amanah's political struggle, but there is room to expand cooperation with other parties that champion values-based politics."

> "In previous state elections, PH worked with Muda, for example in Melaka. Going forward, we want to strengthen PH while opening more space for cooperation with parties that share similar principles, including those with Islamic and Malay-based platforms."

> "Race-based politics may influence voting behaviour, but it is not sustainable in the long run. Ultimately, people will return to issues that affect their daily lives, such as the cost of living, the economy and education."

## Strategic Position

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **PH's Role** | Remains the cornerstone of Amanah's political struggle |
| **Expansion Goal** | Open cooperation with Islamic and Malay-based platforms sharing similar principles |
| **Decision-Making** | Future political strategies/alliances decided collectively within PH |
| **Past Cooperation** | PH previously worked with Muda (e.g., in Melaka state election) |

## Post-Mortem Focus Areas

- Reviewing political cooperation strategies
- Assessing organisational strength
- Examining impact of identity politics on voting behaviour
- Addressing bread-and-butter issues: cost of living, economy, education
- Acknowledging former PH-led NS government had strong administrative record despite electoral losses

## Event Context

- Statements made after opening the **Penang Amanah Convention**
- Penang CM Chow Kon Yeow also attended
- Adly emphasized Amanah respects the people's mandate from the NS election

## Analysis (CJ-MLK-03) — PIR-10

This is Adly Zahari's most detailed post-NS-loss coalition positioning statement. Key PIR-10 signals:
1. **PH remains core** — no PH breakup; Amanah stays anchored in PH
2. **Open to Islamic/Malay-based platforms** — notably vague; could include AMANAH-friendly elements, but NOT BN-PN (those are rivals). This is about broadening PH's alliance options, not joining the BN-PN wave.
3. **Amanah's electoral weakness quantified** — 0/9 seats in NS (including losing Klawang, now held by WAWASAN's Danni Rais). This pressure drives the "wider cooperation" search.
4. **Identity politics acknowledged as challenge** — Adly concedes race-based politics influenced voting but argues it's unsustainable. Defensive framing after Malay-wave losses.
5. **Muda precedent** — references PH-Muda cooperation in Melaka as a model for future alliances.

Contrast with CVS-MLK-126 (BERNAMA, 2 Aug): that captured "value-based politics" + "8 Aug meeting" but NOT the "Islamic and Malay-based platforms" expansion language or the 0/9 NS result. This NST article provides substantially more granular PIR-10 content.

## Analytical Significance (PIR-05 — the negotiation model)

The **Negeri Sembilan result is the empirical template** for the Melaka BN-PN negotiation:
- NS: BN 18 + PN 7 = 25/36 (69%), two-thirds. BN was the senior partner (18 vs 7).
- Melaka: BN holds 20/28 (71%) — structurally similar BN dominance. A Melaka BN-PN split modeled on NS proportions would be roughly **BN ~17-18, PN ~7-8** of 28 — NOT "equal share" (14).
- This is why PN's "equal share" demand (14 seats) is aggressive relative to the NS precedent (where PN got ~28% of seats despite being the junior partner). The gap between PN's ask (14) and the NS-proportional allocation (~7-8) is the negotiation tension.
- **PN's leverage**: the threat of three-cornered fights if no deal. **BN's leverage**: the solo option (20 seats is a comfortable majority without PN).

Johor shows the de facto pact mechanics: PAS told supporters to vote MCA/MIC without calling it a formal deal — a "soft pact." Melaka may follow this pattern (informal coordination rather than a formal seat-sharing agreement).

## Analyst Notes (CJ-MLK-03)

1. **The informal BN-PAS pact is the documented winning formula (PIR-05).** Johor (11 Jul 2026) and NS (1 Aug 2026) both show: BN wins big, PN/BERSATU wiped out, PAS publicly directs supporters to vote BN. Analysts explicitly call this "similar to former Muafakat Nasional alliance." This is the de facto reactivation of Akmal's proposed UMNO-PAS cooperation — achieved *informally* even though Zahid rejected the *formal* Muafakat revival. The Melaka PRN will almost certainly follow this template: BN dominant, PAS backing BN in Malay seats, BERSATU marginalised.

2. **PH/DAP non-Malay support collapse (PIR-10).** The "continued decline of non-Malay support for PH" — Chinese/Indian voters shifting to MCA/MIC — is the structural trend. In Melaka, PH's 5 seats are 4 DAP (urban Chinese) + 1 AMANAH. If DAP's non-Malay base erodes as in Johor/Sabah, even these DAP urban seats (Ayer Keroh, Kesidang, Kota Laksamana, Bandar Hilir) are at risk — leaving AMANAH (Adly's Bukit Katil) as the sole PH redoubt.

3. **PN wiped out but PAS tactically relevant.** PN as a coalition won 0, but PAS's *instruction* to vote BN materially helped BN. This is the new PN role: PAS as a vote-redirector to BN, not a seat-winner. For Melaka, PN's 3 seats (BERSATU 1, PAS 1, WAWASAN 1) may not hold — but PAS's vote direction could boost BN in Malay-majority seats.

4. **BERSAMA rejected coalition talks (PIR-05).** Rafizi's new party contested alone in Johor (0 seats) and is focusing on Melaka. His refusal to negotiate means BERSAMA is a potential *vote-splitter* in the Melaka PRN — relevant to "seat negotiations among opposition parties expected to be critical." If BERSAMA contests PH-leaning urban seats, it could further divide the anti-BN vote.

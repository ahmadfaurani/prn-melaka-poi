## Key Summary

Umno Youth chief Dr Akmal Saleh challenged DAP Youth to withdraw support for state governments led by BN and push its leaders to dissolve Parliament if serious about testing political strength.

## Key Quotes

> "Don't talk about 'forgetting those who helped you', because everyone knows who helped ensure political stability after the last general election."

> "Don't just talk about pushing to dissolve Parliament, withdraw support from the state governments formed together with BN today as well. Withdraw support in Perak. Withdraw support in Pahang."

> "Let us fight in a gentlemanly manner and return the mandate to the people… If you truly want to test your strength, let's open up the arena. Bring it on." — Facebook post

## Context — DAP Youth positions in BN-led states
- DAP Youth chief **Woo Kah Leong** is an executive councillor in the Perak government
- DAP Youth publicity secretary **Ho Chi Yang** is a backbencher supporting the Pahang government
- Both state governments led by BN with PH involved in administration

## Trigger
Akmal (Melaka exco) responding to DAP Youth calling for PM Anwar to dissolve Parliament and clash with BN at the polls if Umno-led coalition insists on going solo in Johor polls. DAP Youth said Umno should not forget PH's role in "reviving" the party after its worst-ever 2022 GE performance.

## Akmal's Counter on Johor
- No need to "panic" over Johor UMNO's intention to go solo — no agreement that BN and PH would contest state/general elections together
- "Don't look so nervous when Umno wants to go solo"
- Took aim at DAP leaders critical of Umno for not withdrawing support for Melaka government: "At least I submitted my letter of resignation (as an exco), not once but twice. What about (DAP)?"
- Akmal remains Melaka exco after resignation letters rejected by CM Ab Rauf Yusoh

## Analysis Note (CJ-MLK-01)

This is a major PIR-02 data point: Akmal is *exporting his anti-DAP confrontation beyond Melaka* — daring DAP Youth to quit BN-led governments in Perak and Pahang. His "I resigned twice, what about DAP?" line directly weaponises his own Melaka Exco resignation as moral leverage in a *national* DAP confrontation. This shows measurable national electoral impact: Akmal's Melaka drama becomes ammunition in multi-state (Perak, Pahang, Johor) coalition politics. It also reveals his tactical position — he wants BN to go solo ("return the mandate to the people"), consistent with his Unity-Government-exit/Muafakat-Nasional platform rejected by Zahid.

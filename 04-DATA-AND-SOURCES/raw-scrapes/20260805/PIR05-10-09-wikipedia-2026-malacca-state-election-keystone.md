### Overview
The **2026 Malacca state election** will elect members of the **16th Malacca State Legislative Assembly**. Must be held **on or before November 2026**, per clause 19(4) of the Constitution of Malacca, unless dissolved earlier by the Yang di-Pertua Negeri on the CM's advice. Assembly likely dissolved within 3 months; election possibly by **September 2026**.

- Total seats: 28 | Majority needed: 15 | Voting age: 18 (auto registration)
- First-past-the-post, single-member constituencies, 5-year terms

## Analyst Notes (CJ-MLK-03) — KEYSTONE ANALYSIS

### A. PRN Timing is NOW (PIR-05)
The Melaka PRN is due **on or before November 2026**, with the CM disclosing a **120-day window** on 17 May 2026 and Wikipedia assessing "possibly September 2026." As of collection (5 Aug 2026 MYT), the election is **1-3 months away**. All coalition positioning is converging on this contest. The "Ab Rauf pertahan kemenangan BN" / "BN-PN mampu kuasai Melaka" signals (Kosmo, 2 Aug, per CJ-MLK-01) and the Aug 4 admin reshuffle are the immediate pre-election manoeuvres.

### B. The Melaka BN-PH State Coalition has COLLAPSED (PIR-05 / PIR-10)
**This is the most important new finding of the cycle.** In July 2026, the BN-PH Melaka state government coalition — which had mirrored the federal unity government since 2023 — **fractured.** The trigger was a BN+PN constitutional amendment (14 July 2026) to create **7 unelected assembly seats**, passed 23-5 (all BN + both PN vs all 5 PH). DAP withdrew immediately; Adly Zahari (PH state chief) initially stayed then rejoined the PH withdrawal on 16 July, **overriding PM Anwar's plea to defer.** 

Implications:
1. The next PRN will be contested with **PH in opposition** (not in coalition with BN). The 2023 unity-mirror arrangement is dead.
2. The 7-unelected-seats amendment is a **coalition-engineering tool**: BN+PN (23 votes) can appoint 7 more members — a patronage/power-consolidation mechanism that further marginalises PH (5) and locks in a BN+PN working majority even if elected seats slip.
3. Adly Zahari's leadership test: he defied Anwar to withdraw with PH. This is a concrete, dated assertion of his state-PH autonomy (PIR-10) — but it leaves PH (5 seats, DAP-heavy) isolated against a 23-strong BN+PN bloc.

### C. PN Melaka: 3 seats, BERSATU-PAS-WAWASAN split, Radzi Jidin chief (PIR-09)
PN's Melaka assembly presence is now **3 seats, but split across all three of its substantive components**: BERSATU 1 (Sungai Udang), PAS 1 (Rembia — Jailani Khamis), WAWASAN 1 (likely Bemban). The PN Melaka chief since 14 May 2026 is **Radzi Jidin (BERSATU acting deputy president)** — a federal BERSATU figure, "not contested" (likely to be appointed via the new 7-seat mechanism). This mirrors the federal PN pattern: BERSATU holds the formal state leadership post, but PAS holds a seat and WAWASAN holds one — the PAS-BERSATU break is reflected at state level. Mas Ermieyati is not listed as a state leader here (she is MP Masjid Tanah, federal), but her party (BERSATU) holds only 1 of PN's 3 state seats — a weakening of the BERSATU/Mas Ermieyati faction's Melaka footprint.

### D. Jailani Khamis = the Muafakat-PN nexus (PIR-05)
**Muhammad Jailani Khamis** is now PN-PAS ADUN for Rembia (N06). He is ALSO the **Muafakat Nasional (NGO) Malacca state chairman** (per the MN scrape). So a single individual straddles the MN NGO structure and a sitting PN-PAS assembly seat. This is a concrete person-level nexus between the Akmal-driven UMNO-PAS (Muafakat) sentiment and the PN-PAS electoral presence in Melaka. Jailani was a BN/PH figure historically (Rembia was PH-PKR in 2018, won by BN-UMNO in 2021) — his move to PN-PAS embodies the Malay-Muslim realignment.

### E. Adly Zahari's seat (Bukit Katil) is AMANAH's sole state seat (PIR-10)
Adly holds N17 Bukit Katil — PH-AMANAH. This is AMANAH's ONLY state seat (PH = DAP 4 + AMANAH 1). His personal retention is the entirety of AMANAH's Melaka legislative presence. With AMANAH winning 0 seats in NS (2026) and 1 in Melaka (2021, down from 2), the party is electorally marginal; Adly's leadership of state PH (since Aug 2017) rests on this single seat.

### F. Ab Rauf's seat (Tanjung Bidara) (PIR-05)
Ab Rauf holds N02 Tanjung Bidara — the seat he won off Mas Ermieyati (PN's 2021 CM candidate) in 2021. He has been CM since 14 July 2018 (per this page; note: he became CM in 2023 per other sources — the "since 14 July 2018" appears to be his BN leadership tenure, not CM tenure). His incumbent status and the BN 20-seat base make him the front-runner, now reinforced by the PN working relationship (23-vote bloc).

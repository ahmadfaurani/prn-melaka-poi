## Full Content

PETALING JAYA: Whether PAS will be willing to cede more seats to Umno, as it did in Johor and Negeri Sembilan, will be a key question if Barisan Nasional (BN) and Perikatan Nasional (PN) cooperate for the Melaka state election, an analyst says.

Ilham Centre research chief Yusri Ibrahim said BN-PN cooperation in Johor and Negeri Sembilan was easier to manage because Umno was the dominant party, while PAS was willing to play a supporting role by allowing Umno to contest more seats.

However, he said the political landscape in Melaka was markedly different, as both Umno and PAS had reason to argue that they were in the stronger position.

He said that in the 2021 Melaka election, Umno led BN to a landslide victory, winning the majority of seats and forming the state government.

However, he said the situation changed after the 15th general election in November 2022, when BN lost all six parliamentary seats in Melaka. PN won three seats, PAS captured Tangga Batu and Jasin, and Bersatu won Masjid Tanah.

"In fact, if the parliamentary votes were broken down according to state constituencies, PN was far ahead of BN, by 10 seats to four.

"This means that, in the context of Melaka and based on the most recent election, PN is stronger than BN, and PAS is stronger than Umno," he said in a Facebook post.

Yusri said this raised an important question: "Will PAS continue to be as generous in giving up seats as it was in Johor and Negeri Sembilan?

"At the same time, will Umno be willing to concede more seats to PAS when it is the dominant party leading the Melaka government?" he said, adding that the Melaka election would merely serve as a warm-up for the relationship between the two coalitions.

Yesterday, PAS president Abdul Hadi Awang said BN and PN could reach a different type of understanding for the upcoming polls in Melaka despite their convincing victory in the Negeri Sembilan election on Saturday.

He said while BN had 20 seats in the 28-member Melaka assembly, the Umno-led coalition held none of the state's parliamentary seats.

On Sunday, Melaka BN chairman Ab Rauf Yusoh said the coalition was open to cooperating with any party under a BN Plus formula for the state election, but discussions would not necessarily lead to an agreement, as they would depend on each party's strengths.

PAS previously announced its intention to contest 16 of Melaka's 28 state seats, including Tanjung Bidara which is held by Rauf.
